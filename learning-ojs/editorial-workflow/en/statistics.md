# Statistics in OJS 3.5 

Statistics features in OJS 3.5 allow you to track and monitor your outreach, editorial activity, workflow performance, registered users, and more. These metrics can help you better understand how your journal is operating through data. 

In this guideline, you can find details about the types of data available, how they are presented in OJS, and how they may serve you and your journal. 

 

## Why care about statistics? {#why} 

 
The data provided by journal statistics can be useful to editors and journal managers in many ways. 

Outreach statistics can tell you:  

* How many people are reading your journal, and where readers are from  
* Which articles, issues, or topics attract the most attention. 
* Whether readership is growing over time. 
* Whether promotional activities, special issues, or conferences are increasing your readership. 

 

Editorial statistics can be used to: 

* Demonstrate the workload handled by editors, editorial assistants, and reviewers. 
* Identify bottlenecks in the editorial process. 
* Monitor submission trends and plan staffing needs, or needs for further outreach to potential authors. 
* Evaluate reviewer response rates. 
* Track turnaround times from submission to decision and publication. 
* Support funding applications, budget requests, and annual reporting. 

 

## Monitor and Analyze your outreach in OJS {#outreach} 

Monitoring outreach helps you understand the extent of your journal’s reach, assess its visibility and discoverability, and evaluate whether its audience and engagement are growing over time. It shows which content is attracting attention and where engagement is strongest. 

Editors and Journal Managers can view article-, issue-, and journal-level usage data in graph and table formats by navigating to the relevant sections under the Statistics menu.

### Track article views and downloads 

 

Use the Articles menu to view article abstract and file views in both graph and table formats.  

 

*Adjust the timeframe*:  

* You can switch the graph between monthly and daily views. 

 

 

![OJS 3.5 articles menu daily/monthly view](./OJS-articles-daily-monthly-view-3.5.png) 

 

* The calendar icon on the top right corner of your dashboard gives you the option of changing the article view to the Last 30 days, Last 90 days, Last 12 months, All dates, or a Custom Date Range. 

 

 

 

![OJS 3.5 articles menu date range](./OJS-3.5-articles-date-range.png) 

 

*Adjust the scope*: 

You can narrow the results by clicking “Filters” and limiting the data to specific sections or issues. 

 

 

![OJS 3.5 articles menu filters](./OJS-3.5-articles-filters.png) 

 

*Manage the results table*  

 

After adjusting the timeframe and scope, you can view the results in the Article Details table, which includes abstract views, views by galley type, and total views.  

 

The table format will allow you to filter the Total in ascending or descending order. 

 

In the table, you can also use the Search function under Article Details to find statistics for a specific article by title, author, or manuscript ID.

## Monitor your editorial activity and workflow performance 

Tracking editorial activity may help journals understand how efficiently the editorial workflow is functioning. It provides evidence on submission volume, editorial decisions, review and publication timelines, and overall processing outcomes. This supports more informed planning and helps identify operational issues that may affect editorial quality, timeliness, and journal growth. 

 

Under Editorial Activity statistics, you can view a graph showing all active submissions, including the total number of active submissions and their breakdown by stage. 

 

![OJS 3.5 editorial activity report](./OJS-3.5-editorial-activity-report.png) 

 

The Trends table shows the number of submissions received, accepted, declined, and published, as well as editorial workflow statistics such as acceptance and rejection rates, including further breakdowns. 

The Trends table can be filtered by timeframe and by section. 

 

![OJS 3.5 editorial activity trends](./OJS-3.5-editorial-activity-trends.png) 

 

There are some important things to note when considering the data in these reports: 

* Published submissions within a date range only count the initial publication date and not subsequent versions. 

* Days to decision is the number of days that it takes 80% of submissions to receive an editorial decision.  

* The acceptance and decline rates only count submissions that have received an accept/decline decision, so they exclude active submissions that have not reached a final decision. 

* If a date range is applied, it only counts submissions that were submitted AND accepted/declined within that date range. So, e.g., a submission that was submitted before the date range but received an accept/reject decision within the date range will not be counted. For this reason, it’s best to use long date ranges and older date ranges to get accurate acceptance/rejection rates. 

An Editorial Activity Report will be generated monthly and can be sent by email to editors and section editors. All managers and section editors are automatically opted out of the monthly report; however, you can opt in by going to User Profile > Notifications and unchecking the box that disables the automated email. 

To enable or disable the Editorial statistics for all editors, navigate to Settings > Workflow > Emails > Editorial statistics. 

 

## Monitor Registered Users 

User statistics provide information about registered users in the journal. By exporting the user list, you can generate a user report that includes ID, given name, family name, email, phone, country, mailing address, date registered, and enabled roles.  

The Total column is not a total number of accounts created; it identifies how many users have that role in the system right now. When an existing user receives a new role, such as a registered author becoming a reviewer, that addition to the total number of reviewers in the system will be reflected in the data by an increase in the number of reviewers but no change in the total number of users.

## Counter R5 Reports 

OJS includes [COUNTER Code of Practice](https://cop5.projectcounter.org/en/5.0.3/00-foreword.html) integration, making usage data available through COUNTER R5 reports. 

This COUNTER Code of Practice provides a framework for the recording and exchange of online usage statistics for the major categories of e-resources (journals, databases, books, reference works, and multimedia databases) at an international level. 

The system generates reports that track the details associated with site usage and submissions over a given period of time. These reports can be broadly grouped into “usage” reports that contain usage metrics indicative of visitor readership and “content” reports that provide data on the respective item (eg. review information). Reports are usually generated in CSV format which requires a spreadsheet application to view. 

* Review Report: Provides review information on all articles in the system, including reviewer names, reviews, and recommendations. (Content) 

* Articles Report: Provides a spreadsheet of all published articles. (Content) 

* Subscriptions Report: Provides information on any individual and institutional subscriptions. This report option is only available if subscriptions are enabled on the journal. (Content) 

* COUNTER Reports: Provides COUNTER 5 reports for all journals on the OJS application. Provides monthly and year-to-date aggregate counts for abstract and galley views. (See the [Admin Guide](https://docs.pkp.sfu.ca/admin-guide/en/statistics.html) for information on accessing Counter R5 reports.) (Usage) 

 

## Glossary & key definitions 

These definitions can help you understand precisely which pages and files are being tracked, and how different views are tracked by OJS. 

* Abstract page, article page, landing page: The main page for a single article, containing the title, author information, abstract, DOI, and links to galleys. Generally, article DOIs and links point to this abstract page, as opposed to galley files. 

* Article view: Refers to a single unique view of the article abstract page by a visitor. This is not an aggregate count of all article and galley views - it refers to *abstract views only*. 

* Galley view, galley download: A single unique view or download of a specific galley file by a visitor. This is not an aggregate count of all galley views - if there are multiple galleys for an article (e.g., an HTML file, a PDF file, etc.), each will have its own unique galley view count. OJS does not distinguish between “downloads” and “views”. 

* Multi-clicks: Accidentally or intentionally increasing usage counts by rapidly clicking on an abstract page or galley file multiple times. OJS identifies and removes multi-clicks from its metrics, as per the Project COUNTER Code of Practice.

* Robots, crawlers, bots: Nonhuman site visitors who may still view and download article data. These are usually identified as such to the server, and OJS does not count them in its usage metrics, as per the Project COUNTER Code of Practice. 

* [Project COUNTER Code of Practice](https://www.countermetrics.org/code-of-practice/): Developed by COUNTER, the Code provides rules on what should be counted as a view, including specific rules for robot usage and multi-click abuse. OJS adheres to these rules when counting metrics.
